\subsection{Real-World Recommender Systems: Constraints and Objectives}

Recommender systems have seen significant evolutions across various domains
like e-commerce, media streaming, education, and social platforms.
\cite{zou2025surveyrealworldrecommendersystems}
Early studies in this field were more directed towards raw recommendations
going through algorithmic accuracy; recent industrial requirements have 
shaped this around deployment constraints.

Application of Recommender Systems in industrial environments highlights 
several new dimensions of challenges: massive user populations, continuously 
evolving item catalogs, strict latency requirements, and business-driven 
optimization goals.
\cite{zou2025surveyrealworldrecommendersystems}
These implementations are built over several stages like candidate generation, 
ranking, filtering, and post-processing components.
Each stage suffers from a trade-off between computational efficiency and 
predictive performance.

Earlier, most recommender systems heavily relied on measurable metrics like 
click-through rate (CTR), watch time, or interaction frequency.
These metrics are easy-to-use because developing a testing and experimentation
system around these metrics is relatively easier and quicker to deploy.
However, these signals are imperfect representations of user satisfaction
on a long-term value due to several noise factors like fatigue and addiction.
Therefore, implementations based on these metrics maximize short-term 
measurable engagement rather than user benefit and welfare.
\cite{zou2025surveyrealworldrecommendersystems}

\subsection{Traditional and Sequential Recommendation Paradigms}

The foundations of recommender systems originate from 
collaborative filtering and content-based recommendation methods.
These algorithms modelled on user preferences 
using historical interaction patterns.
Matrix factorization techniques introduced latent representations that enabled
scalable personalization and became the dominant paradigm for many years.

With the rise of deep learning algorithms, recommendation shifted toward neural
ranking models capable of learning complex relationships between users and items.
This approach improved predictive performance but worked around the 
same objective: estimating the likelihood of immediate interaction.

By introducing temporal dynamics into the system, 
gave rise to sequential recommendation models.
Architectures such as recurrent neural networks (e.g., GRU4Rec) and 
attention-based transformer models (e.g., SASRec and subsequent variants)
treat user interaction histories as ordered sequences.
These systems can learn recency effects and temporal dependencies, 
which can further enhance next-item predictions.

Despite their success, sequential recommenders rely on a key assumption:
observed interactions represent positive utility signals.
Since the objective still remains the same, that is, next-click or next-
interaction prediction; this implicitely assumes that maximizing immediate
engagement corresponds to maximizing user satisfaction.
Consequently, users are modeled as behavioral sequences, 
ignoring internal states such as fatigue and boredom. 
This also neglects the natural formation of preference.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning for Long-Term Recommendation}

Reinforcement learning (RL) mechanisms are well known to be built around the 
optimization goal of acquiring long term rewards; which seem to fit perfectly 
with the problem of algorithms converging towards a short-term goal issue.
Therefore RL has been introduced as a framework for recommendation to address 
these temporal dependencies beyond immediate prediction.
RL formulations treat recommendation as a sequential decision process in which the
system selects actions (recommendations) to maximize cumulative reward over time.

Early approaches focused on contextual bandits, which balance exploration
and exploitation while optimizing short-term feedback signals.
Recent work extends this framework to long-horizon optimization, modeling delayed
rewards such as retention, session continuation, or user lifetime value.
\cite{Rastogi2025MultiScaleCB}

RL also enables reward shaping, allowing designers to encode
multiple objectives into a single optimization signal.
However, existing RL recommender systems inherit a critical 
limitation: reward definitions remain engagement-centric.
When modeling long-term outcomes, rewards are often derived 
from interaction frequency or platform usage duration.
As a result, RL improves temporal awareness without 
fundamentally redefining the optimization objective.
\cite{Bolis2025BalancingBA,Anwar2024RecommendationAT}

Studies also introduce addiction-aware recommendations, which further highlight 
this issue, showing that maximizing engagement-based rewards can unintentionally
encourage compulsive consumption patterns.
Thus, RL provides a mechanism for long-term optimization 
but does not guarantee alignment with user wellbeing.
\cite{Bolis2025BalancingBA,xia2026llmenhancedreinforcementlearninglongterm,Anwar2024RecommendationAT}

\subsection{Generative and LLM-Based Recommender Systems}

The recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have started a major a paradigm 
shift in most data science fields, including recommender systems.
The use of LLMs in recommender systems introduces generative and reasoning-based
recommendation, while moving away from discriminative ranking.
\cite{yu2026thinkrecthinkingbasedrecommendationllm}
Generative recommendation frameworks take recommendations as a 
sequence generation or reasoning task rather than a scoring problem.
\cite{hou2025surveygenerativerecommendationdata}

LLM-based approaches offer a deeper insight about the 
user preferences and natural language interaction. 
This approach also offers improved cold-start handling 
through pretrained knowledge representations.
\cite{202512.0741}
Agent-based recommender architectures further extend this idea by allowing 
reasoning loops, planning behaviors, and iterative refinement of recommendations.
\cite{zhang2023agentcfcollaborativelearningautonomous,shang2025agentrecbenchbenchmarkingllmagentbased}
Explainable recommendation systems also benefit from LLMs'
ability to produce human-interpretable justifications.
\cite{park2026more}


Despite these advances, the primary optimization objective remains unchanged.
LLMs enhance how recommendations are generated but rarely reconsider what should be optimized.
Most systems still aim to maximize predicted relevance or engagement likelihood.
\cite{202512.0741,liang2026generativereasoningreranker}

\subsection{Cognitive and Agentic Recommendation Frameworks}

Emerging researches explore recommender systems
capable of reflection, memory, and reasoning.
System-2 recommender frameworks try to separate impulsive engagement
from reflective utility by modeling decision-capable processes.
\cite{Agarwal2024System2RD}

Agent-based architectures introduce persistent memory structures, 
reasoning cycles, and iterative refinement mechanisms.
\cite{yang2026graphbasedagentmemorytaxonomy}
These frameworks incorporates reasoning, reflection, and refinement 
attempt to simulate decision-making processes beyond simple ranking.
\cite{gu2025r4ecreasoningreflectionrefinement}

These approaches represent an important conceptual shift: recommendation 
is framed as a cognitive process rather than a prediction problem.
Memory-aware agents and reasoning loops allow systems to reconsider
their previous decisions and incorporate broader understanding.
\cite{yang2026graphbasedagentmemorytaxonomy}

However, existing cognitive recommender research primarily focuses 
on reasoning capability, rather than human state modeling.
While systems have become more reflective internally, they rarely model 
user fatigue, burden, or diminishing utility explicitly.
\cite{Carroll2025CTRLRecCR}

\subsection{Human-Centered Recommendation and Welfare Optimization}

Some researches have questioned whether engagement-maximizing 
goals align with human welfare.
Studies demonstrate that optimizing for interaction metrics can 
amplify biases, and encourage excessive consumption behaviors.
\cite{Milli2023EngagementUS,Anwar2024RecommendationAT}

Works on recommendation and temptation explores how exposure to engagement 
oriented recommendations can influence a users self-control and decision making.
Similarly, research on welfare-aware optimization investigates trade-offs between
platform performance and user wellbeing; highlighting the tension between
short-term engagement and long-term satisfaction.
\cite{Milli2023EngagementUS,Wang2025LowerBH}

Recommender models which work with addiction awareness, incorporates 
risk-sensitive objectives that penalize harmful interaction patterns.
Studies on bias and fairness further reveal that recommendation 
embeddings can encode sensitive associations (often personal).
\cite{Wang2025LowerBH,Beattie2025UnderstandingSA}

Collectively, these works argue that recommender systems should be evaluated not 
only by accuracy but also by their long-term societal and psychological effects.
\cite{dignazi2025rewardingengagementpersonalizationpopularitybased}

\subsection{Evaluation Challenges and Control Mechanisms}

Depending on what aspects a model is evaluated, heavily 
shapes around the internal designing of that system.
Traditional metrics such as $NDCG$, $Recall@K$, and $Mean Reciprocal Rank$ 
measure ranking accuracy under static assumptions.
While effective for benchmarking predictive performance,
these metrics fail to capture long-term effects.

Recent research highlights inconsistencies in evaluation protocols, 
particularly for explainability and reasoning methods.
Offline evaluation often fails to predict online performance due to 
feedback loops and distribution shifts introduced due to deployed circumstances.
\cite{10.1145/3705328.3748028}

Approaches built around control, like conformal risk control, attempt to
mitigate unwanted recommendations, by introducing uncertainty measures.
Studies comparing offline and online evaluation further emphasize that
optimizing standard metrics can reinforce short-term optimization biases.
\cite{detoni2025dontbringflowersmitigating}

\subsection{Literature Synthesis and Identified Research Gap}

Across the surveyed literature, recommender system research has progressed along multiple
independent trajectories: improved sequential modeling, long-term optimization via 
reinforcement learning, reasoning-enabled recommendation through LLMs, and growing 
awareness of human welfare implications.
However, these advances remain largely disconnected.
Table~\ref{tab:collective-findings} highlights the gap between existing capabilities 
and the components that are still missing for human-centered recommendation systems.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Overview of current capabilities and what is still missing\label{tab:collective-findings}}
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{|l||Y||Y|}
\hline
Capability & Existing Work & Missing Component \\
\hline
Sequential modeling & Accurate behavioral prediction & Cognitive state awareness \\
RL optimization & Long-term interaction modeling & Human-aligned reward formulation \\
LLM reasoning & Semantic understanding and reflection & Adaptive decision control \\
Welfare research & Identification of harms & Operational system integration \\
Evaluation research & Metric critique and control methods & Alignment with wellbeing objectives \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

This reveals some patterns like: the individual components necessary for human-centered
recommendation exist, yet no unified framework integrates cognitive modeling, adaptive reasoning, 
fatigue awareness, and long-term optimization, all put into a single recommender architecture.
\cite{zou2025surveyrealworldrecommendersystems,Bolis2025BalancingBA,hou2025surveygenerativerecommendationdata,Agarwal2024System2RD,dignazi2025rewardingengagementpersonalizationpopularitybased,Anwar2024RecommendationAT,10.1145/3705328.3748028}

This gap motivates the proposed Dual-System Human-Centric Recommender with Fatigue-Aware 
Long-Term Optimization (DS-HCRS), which aims to bridge behavioral prediction, cognitive modeling, 
and long-term welfare optimization within a unified framework.
