While the proposed Dual-System Human-Centric Recommender System introduces several ideas for improving recommendation quality and user experience, it also has limitations that must be acknowledged.

One limitation arises from the \textbf{difficulty of accurately modeling user cognitive states}.
The fatigue estimation proposed in this work relies on observable interaction signals such as repetition, novelty, session length, and interaction speed.
However, these signals are only indirect indicators of a user's internal cognitive state.
As a result, the fatigue model may sometimes misinterpret user behavior or fail to capture more complex psychological factors that influence user engagement.

Another limitation concerns the \textbf{evaluation of long-term outcomes}.
Many recommender systems are evaluated using offline datasets that contain historical interactions.
While such datasets allow controlled experimentation, they do not always capture the dynamic feedback loops that occur in real-world deployments.
Long-term effects such as user retention, boredom, or satisfaction may therefore be difficult to measure accurately without large-scale online experiments.

The proposed system also introduces additional \textbf{computational complexity}.
Combining multiple components-including sequential recommendation models, fatigue estimation, reinforcement learning optimization, and gating mechanisms-may increase system training and inference costs.
Efficient implementation and system scalability would therefore require careful engineering in real-world deployments.

Beyond technical limitations, recommender systems raise important \textbf{ethical considerations}.
Recommendation algorithms influence what users see, consume, and engage with on digital platforms.
If designed poorly, they may contribute to issues such as content echo chambers, amplification of harmful material, or excessive platform usage.

The proposed framework attempts to address some of these concerns by incorporating fatigue awareness and long-term user utility into the recommendation process.
However, designing recommender systems that balance platform goals, user satisfaction, and societal impact remains an open challenge.
Future research should explore how human-centered recommendation objectives can be integrated with fairness, transparency, and responsible AI principles.
